01 / THE STORY

More than a
drop of fat.

Lipid droplets (LDs) were once mainly viewed as structures for storing excess fat. Research has shown that they also help cells manage energy, maintain membranes and respond to infection.

During viral infection these functions can pull in opposite directions: host cells can use LDs to strengthen antiviral defence, while viruses can exploit the same organelles to support replication.

“Not automatically pro-virus or pro-host — their effect depends on who controls them.”

03 / WHY IT MATTERS

A tiny organelle.
A big therapeutic question.

Because many viruses depend on host lipid pathways, understanding lipid droplets may reveal ways to interfere with viral replication without targeting the virus directly. The challenge is preserving the essential cellular functions these pathways also perform.
